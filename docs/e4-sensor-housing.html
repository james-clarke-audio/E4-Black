Sensor Housing Geometry

The printed housing measured from the STL, and the optics it sets up: a narrow SFH 4550 emitter over an OP505A phototransistor, with the detector bore tilted 5° up so its cone converges on the emitter's spot in the lower half of the wall. Black resin — no IR bleed through the septum.

Height by legs, angle by bore. Bending legs reliably sets how high the pair sits (to drop the beams into the wall's lower half), but it can't hold a stable tilt — in a snug bore the part follows the bore. So the 5° convergence lives in the print (identical on all four sensors), and you keep bending legs for height. Tilting the detector up rather than the emitter down keeps the bright, narrow emitter spot safely off the floor at this low ~7 mm mount.
Seat flush, never proud. The 8 mm bore only shrouds a device that sits inside it. If an emitter pokes proud of the front face, its dome side‑spill escapes the barrel and skims straight into the detector — a bright, wall‑like reading in open air (covering the emitter drops it to zero). Push each dome flush with, or a few tenths recessed behind, the front face, and seat all four to the same depth so one threshold fits. The emitter is forgiving of depth — the Ø5.8 bore and ±3° beam clear the aperture even a couple of mm back, so err recessed. A proud detector defeats the shroud the same way (a wider field that drinks in stray light and crosstalk), but don't over‑recess it either: past ~2 mm the Ø4 bore begins to vignette its ±10° acceptance and you lose reflected signal. Flush to a hair recessed is its sweet spot.

If you want to tune it

The 5° bore crosses the two axes at about 73 mm (6.4 mm ÷ tan 5°). That centres the response on the mid‑to‑far range; a side wall at ~50 mm sits just inside the crossing, and the detector's ±10° cone still catches the spot there. If you'd rather centre exactly on the ~50 mm side‑wall distance, ~7° does it (crossing ≈ 52 mm) — say the word and I'll regenerate the STL at whatever angle you want.
