E4 Micromouse · position integrity

Staying Located

A micromouse navigates by dead-reckoning — it counts wheel turns to know where it is. But wheels slip and scrub, so that estimate drifts. This chapter is how the E4 keeps its believed position honest: where it starts, and how the walls re-anchor it as it runs.

The start reference

She starts in cell (0,0), the SW corner, facing north (heading 0), placed back-edge flat against the back wall — a hand-start.

The back wall pins two of the three degrees of freedom: it squares her heading (both rear corners touch, so she aligns to the cell axis) and gives a hard longitudinal zero. It does not fix her lateral position — placement (or the start cell's side wall) has to centre her left-to-right.

The geometry, measured on the mouse (all distances from the cell's back boundary line):

QuantityValueMeaning
Wall face6 mma 12 mm wall straddles the boundary, so its inner face sits 6 mm into the cell
Rear → axle35 mmfrom the mouse's back edge to the axle / centre of rotation
Axle at start41 mm6 + 35 — where the axle sits when she's against the wall (POSE_START_Y)
Cell centre90 mmhalf of the 180 mm cell pitch (HALF_CELL)
BACK_WALL_TO_CENTER49 mm90 − 41 — the forward travel to put the axle on centre

That 49 mm reads two ways, which is a handy sanity check: it's the distance the axle travels from the wall to the cell centre, and the clearance from her back edge to the wall once she's centred. Sit her on the alignment jig's centre cross and a ruler from back edge to wall reads 49 mm — if it does, the whole chain is confirmed.

On go (hand-start) she drives the 49 mm forward, then declares set_position(HALF_CELL) — she now knows she's at the centre of cell 0. The auto-start variant instead expects her placed at the centre and reverses into the wall to square herself first; the placement and the m_handStart flag must agree, or she'll try to reverse into a wall she's already touching.

Between cells — dead reckoning, and its drift

Once running, the E4 currently tracks position by odometry alone. There is no sensor-based correction wired in yet:

  • adjustPosition() is an empty hook ({ return; }) — the designated home for per-cell correction, not yet filled.
  • The front-sensor turn trigger is off — turns fire on wheel distance, not on seeing the wall (get_front_sum() > 0, "distance only").
  • FRONT_REFERENCE = 850 (the front-sum when centred on a wall ahead) is defined but unused.

So between references her belief accumulates wheel error — slip, and the skid-steer scrub of the tetra chassis — with nothing correcting it mid-run. Left unchecked that's what makes a mouse turn a few millimetres early, clip a post, or mis-map a cell.

The scrub is now measured. Eight in-place 90° spins during a gyro-scale run moved the dead-reckoned pose from (90, 209) to (72, 192) — about 25 mm of translation, ~3 mm per 90° turn. Two wheels a side off one pinion cannot pivot cleanly about the axle cross, so every stop-and-turn ends a few millimetres off where the brain thinks she is. That is not a fault to fix in firmware; it is the budget the side-sensor centring has to absorb, and it is the strongest argument for a positional re-anchor every cell rather than only where a front wall offers one.

Three re-anchors

The plan to fill adjustPosition() — three ways the walls hand back a known position:

Front-wall snap Planned

When a wall is ahead and the front-sum reaches FRONT_REFERENCE, she's a known distance from it → snap her longitudinal position to truth.

Side-edge — "wall vanishes" Planned

The side sensors aim at the flat wall face just inboard of the (slotted) post, for a steady reflection. As the wall's trailing edge passes, the reading falls through a threshold at a fixed odometry count → snap. Calibrate the count empirically; guard the snap to a plausible window (reject a missing wall / glitch); blend, don't jump, at speed. A reference every cell, even with no wall ahead.

Dead-end re-square Planned

At a dead end, after the 180 she reverses into the wall now behind her → re-zeroes both heading and longitudinal position. A dead end hands you a squaring wall for free.

Turn strategy — search vs speed run

The per-cell reference depends on the side sensors watching the walls go by — but an integrated (arc) turn flies through the corner cell with the side sensors off, so that cell gets no reference of its own. The resolution is to use a different turn style in each phase:

  • Search — stop-and-turn. Every cell, corner cells included, is stopped in, sensed, mapped and referenced. Search is about a correct map, so it trades pace for a reference in every cell.
  • Speed run — integrated turns. The map is already known from the search, so the flown-through corner cell needn't be sensed. The turn is bracketed by the wall-vanish reference on the approach straight (starts from truth) and the one on the exit straight (re-corrects immediately) — with the gyro-calibrated angle in between.
Future work: integrated turns during search — faster mapping, at the cost of the weaker corner referencing. One to revisit once everything beneath it is solid.

Where it stands

  • Built: the start reference (back-wall geometry, verified) and odometry tracking through the run.
  • Planned: the three re-anchors that fill adjustPosition(), and dead-end re-square — all of which lean on the side sensors working, which is why their aim and calibration matter for the run, not just the map.
