E4 Micromouse · design note · not built

Next Board — G431 Front End

A forward-looking design note, not a record of what exists. The competing mouse is the STM32F411 build documented in the rest of this manual; the STM32G431 is the next-generation platform. This captures how its sensor front end should be built, and why two tempting external parts were ruled out.

Timing reality: the G431 lands mid-October and the competition is in October — so this is a post-competition project. The F411 mouse is what runs. Kept here so the thinking isn't lost, not as a distraction from finishing the mouse that has a date.

Where the idea came from

The starting question was whether to boost detector sensitivity in hardware. Two routes were considered, both drawn from a university micromouse build that used the same OP505A detector behind a discrete darlington gain stage.

That original design is the right shape: it keeps a fast phototransistor as the light-sensing element and puts the gain in a separate stage behind it. There's a bonus — feeding the phototransistor into a low-impedance darlington input unloads it, removing the Miller-loading of a large collector resistor, so it can be faster as well as higher-gain. That is not the same thing as a photodarlington, where the slowness is in the sensing die itself.

The proposed signal chain

detector → load resistor → on-chip op-amp (PGA) → ADC
  ↳ ADC conversion timer-triggered, synchronised to the emitter pulse
  ↳ whole dark/lit 4-channel sequence run by DMA, no CPU
  ↳ hardware oversampling for SNR
  ↳ comparator on a side channel → hardware capture of the encoder count on the wall-vanish edge
  • Per-channel gain in firmware. The PGA setting is software, so channel-to-channel mismatch — the hand-soldered aim differences fought throughout this build — can be normalised in code rather than with matched resistors and an iron.
  • Sequential emitters make three op-amps enough for four channels. Only one emitter is lit at a time, so no four simultaneous chains are needed: either one shared amp behind an analog mux, or three on-chip plus one channel straight to the ADC. Decide this at schematic time, not at layout.
  • Comparators serve the position plan. Rather than polling the ADC for a threshold crossing, a comparator can trigger a hardware capture of the encoder count at the side-sensor falling edge — a far more precise longitudinal landmark at speed than software polling. See Ch 7 · Staying Located.

Ruled out — and why

OP535A photodarlington detector Rejected

Roughly 15–30× more sensitive than the OP505A (10.5–32 mA at 0.13 mW/cm² vs 4.3–5.95 mA at 0.50 mW/cm²), and a mechanical drop-in. Rejected on three counts: it would saturate the ADC at close range — worse now the TSAL6100 emitter is spectrally matched to the ~935 nm detector and signal has gone up; a darlington sensing die is slow, capping the read rate a running mouse needs; and its distance response is less linear, which hurts steering and edge detection. Sensitivity was never the bottleneck.

SMP04 sample-and-hold Rejected

Its value is capturing the detector at the peak of the emitter pulse. The G431 does that natively: trigger the ADC from a timer synchronised to the pulse — hardware-timed, jitter-free, DMA-fed. An external sample-and-hold adds parts and an extra analog hop for no gain. (It is also an ageing part — check stock before designing anything around it.)

External darlington gain stage Superseded

Sound technique, and the inspiration for this note — but the on-chip PGA does the same job with firmware-settable, per-channel gain, which fixed hardware cannot. Keep the idea in reserve if the on-chip op-amps prove short on bandwidth or noise performance in practice.

Open questions for schematic time

  • Shared amp behind a mux, or three op-amps plus one direct ADC channel?
  • Load-resistor value re-picked for the PGA input range — the current 1.8 k was chosen for a bare ADC input.
  • How much of the gain budget to take as PGA gain versus oversampling — oversampling adds no analog noise, so favour it first.
  • Whether the comparator edge-capture replaces or merely supplements the firmware threshold detection.
