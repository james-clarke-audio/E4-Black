Micromouse · IR wall sensing

Aiming the E4's wall sensors

Matched to your build: the inner diagonal pair (SL/SR) looks out at the side walls, the outer pair (FL/FR) looks forward at the wall ahead. Rough targets to dial in on the bench — beam patterns vary, so treat the angles as ballpark.

Rough target angles

  • Inner pairSL · SR → sides
    ≈30–45° out from forward, aimed to graze the side wall / leading post ~40–70 mm ahead of the nose. This is your left/right wall + opening detection and steering error.
  • Outer pairFL · FR → front
    ≈forward, ~5–15° splay. Both catch the wall ahead; FL + FR summed is your front distance, and the small splay helps front-wall squareness.
  • Toe-inper pair
    a few ° — nudge each emitter and its detector onto the same spot so their cones overlap at working distance (~40–90 mm).

Side view — a 50 mm band, and a floor 7 mm below you

Correction — 14 Sep 2026. This section previously called the wall a “12 mm band” and told you to aim level or a hair down. Both were wrong: 12 mm is the wall’s thickness, not its height, and taking it for the height inverted the advice.

Run the numbers from 7 mm up. To put the beam on the floor you need only atan(7/d) — about 2° at one cell ahead and barely 1° at two, which is well inside the scatter of a hand-aimed mount. To clear the top of the wall instead you need atan(43/d): 13° at one cell, 7° at two. The errors are nowhere near symmetric.

The two failures aren’t equal either. Over the top, an absent wall reads as absent and a present wall is simply missed. Into the floor, every cell returns something, the open-floor reading climbs to meet the wall reading, and the margin the threshold calibration depends on collapses — which is the same failure a white or marble bench top produces, from the other direction. Aim level, and bias up rather than down.

Why this arrangement works

  • Diagonals on the post = the best steering signal. Aimed forward-out at the wall/post, SL and SR give a clean lateral error for wall-following and spot a side opening before she's on top of it — plus a sharp peak as she passes each post, a natural cell-boundary reference.
  • Forward pair for the wall ahead. FL + FR summed is your front distance; the small splay between them helps tell if you're square, which is what you'll want for front-wall centring later.
  • Mind direct crosstalk. Keep a little separation (or a rib) between each emitter and its detector so IR can't leak straight across without hitting a wall. Ambient subtraction kills daylight, not optical crosstalk.

Where the numbers come from now

The thresholds are no longer read off by eye and typed into the source. There are three of them — WALL_THRESH_LEFT, WALL_THRESH_RIGHT and WALL_THRESH_FRONT, the last tested against the FL + FR sum — and a guided routine on the mouse (menu Threshold cal, BT key T) measures all three from three captures:

  • Dead end — both sides and the front all present.
  • Open floor — nothing in range in any direction.
  • Corridor — sides present, front absent. This is the state she spends most of her life in, and two captures cannot describe it: it is the case that decides whether the front channel is being judged against the right absent-state.

Each capture is a burst of fifteen samples reduced by median, not mean — one stray reflection is discarded rather than quietly averaged in. The threshold itself is the midpoint, but the number that decides whether any of this works is the margin: the separation between weakest-present and strongest-absent, reported per channel as a percentage of the present reading. A threshold sitting in a wide gap survives battery sag, a warm emitter and a different floor; one in a narrow gap flips on all three, and no tuning fixes a sensor that cannot tell the two states apart. The routine also warns if the two sides differ by more than 25 % — that is a mount fault, and no threshold will hide it.

Open for measurement. Only the open-floor capture has been taken on the rebuilt sensors so far — SL 16, SR 15, FL 15, FR 7, with the FL channel steady at a standard deviation of 1.02 where it had been swinging past 90. The dead-end and corridor captures are still to come, and they have to be taken on a maze floor section, not a bench top: the competition floor is black and absorbs, and a white or marble surface returns floor bounce that will sit the absent-state readings far too high and set every threshold with it. This section gets its real numbers once those two captures exist.

Angles are ballpark — narrow-beam parts want tighter aim and reach further; wide-beam parts are more forgiving but noisier. The fitted parts are TSAL6100 emitters (940 nm) into OP505A phototransistors.
